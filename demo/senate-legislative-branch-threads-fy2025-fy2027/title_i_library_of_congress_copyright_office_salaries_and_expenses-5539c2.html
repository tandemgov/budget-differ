Language as of FY2027

revised FY2027The United States Copyright Office [USCO and Copyright Office] plays an important role in our Nation's intellectual property system. Copyright law is the foundation for the creation and dissemination of American artistic works, leading to substantial economic and cultural benefits. The Copyright Office serves multiple central roles in the copyright system, overseeing the registration of copyrighted works, administering the copyright law, and providing counsel to Congress on copyright policy. The Committee recommends the direct appropriation of $56,889,000 for the Copyright Office, and approves authority to spend receipts of $49,569,000, for a total of $106,458,000 in fiscal year 2027. The recommendation for total authority is $4,072,000 above the fiscal year 2026 appropriation.
added FY2027Copyright Collections Authority.--The Committee is aware that the Copyright Office has recently received public comments in response to their Notice of Proposed Rulemaking [NOPR] on changes to its fee schedule and is actively reviewing those comments and drafting a report to submit to Congress. The Committee has included an increase in the Copyright Office's offsetting collections authority; however, it is conditional on the fee schedule increase being finalized, approved, and implemented in a timely fashion. The Committee directs the Copyright Office to keep it apprised of any developments that may have budgetary effects during this administrative period.
added FY2027Implementation of the Music Modernization Act.--The Orrin G. Hatch-Bob Goodlatte Music Modernization Act [MMA], signed in 2018, is crucial for updating U.S. copyright law to the digital streaming era. It simplifies how streaming services license music, ensures songwriters and artists receive royalties for pre-1972 recordings, and provides a legal method for producers to collect royalties. The USCO should prioritize finalizing their review of the designation of the mechanical licensing collective.
added FY2027Artificial Intelligence and Copyright Protections for Songwriters and Artists.--The Committee acknowledges the growing impact of AI on the creation and distribution of music and other copyrighted works, and is concerned that certain Al systems may use copyrighted material without proper authorization, thereby undermining the value of intellectual property. Therefore, the Committee directs the USCO to provide to the Committees on Appropriations, Judiciary, and Commerce not later than 90 days after enactment of this act, a congressional briefing on avoiding disincentives for creation of original creative works and ensuring payment of fair market value for use of intellectual property in the age of AI, including an analysis of existing copyright licensing frameworks and market-based licensing alternatives in relation to AI model training and output.
added FY2027Copyright Royalty Board.--The Committee directs any current or future official, office, or entity vested with supervisory, administrative, regulatory, or oversight authority over the Copyright Royalty Board [CRB] to promptly implement regulations to ensure the CRB operates efficiently and predictably, which shall include timely determinations of controversies, commencement of distribution proceedings within 3 years of royalty claims (with consolidation of multi-year disputes), and resolution of motions and petitions within 6 months.

Information Technology Continuous Development.--The Committee recommends funding for Copyright Office IT continuous development. The Committee directs the Copyright Office and the Library's Office of Chief Information Officer [OCIO] to continue to work together to achieve efficiencies in shared services, while allowing for mission specific continued improvements to be the responsibility of the Copyright Office. The Committee directs the Copyright Office to provide an updated detailed report within 60 days of enactment of the act for the IT continuous development efforts intended to be addressed with the funds provided in fiscal year 2025 and the funds utilization to date. The report should provide detailed information on how these efforts align to the requirements of the communities being served by the Copyright Office.
contains: mandatory direction, reporting requirement, deadline
Artificial Intelligence Impacts on Copyrights.--The Committee recognizes the increasingly important role of Artificial Intelligence [AI] and the potential impact this technology will have on the creation and use of new and existing copyrighted materials. Therefore, the Committee strongly encourages the Copyright Office to continue on an expedited basis with its AI initiative to support its critical roles in administering the Copyright Act and advising Congress and others on copyright law and policy. The Library is directed to brief the Committee within 90 days of enactment of this act on its continued efforts.
Independence for the Purpose of Issuing Copyright Decisions.--The Committee continues to support the independence of the Copyright Office from the Library of Congress for the purposes of consideration and issuance of copyright decisions.
