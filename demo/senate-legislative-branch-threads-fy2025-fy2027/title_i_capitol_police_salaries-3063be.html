 FY2027 substantive

Language as of FY2027

figures updated FY2027The amount provided covers salaries, benefits, overtime costs and salaries related incentives programs to recruit and retain highly qualified personnel. The Committee recommends $720,084,000 for USCP salaries, which is $66,662,000 above the fiscal year 2026 appropriation.
revised FY2026USCP Office of Inspector General.--The Committee notes that as the USCP has grown over the last several fiscal years, so has the workload of the USCP OIG. To support the oversight of an expanding agency, the Committee includes funds to support not less than 19 FTEs within the USCP OIG and includes no less than $897,000 in operating funds from within the USCP's annual appropriation.
revised FY2027Overtime.--The Committee recommends no more than $80,837,000 for overtime in fiscal year 2027 without notification. This provides for approximately 934,425 hours of additional duty. The Committee notes that overtime pay is a standard practice law enforcement entities utilize to respond to unscheduled and unanticipated events. However, understanding the impact of overtime on the onboard personnel, the Committee finds that keeping overtime pay at a minimum should continue to be a goal of the USCP. The Committee directs the USCP to continue its efforts to better track and manage overtime pay in order to keep overtime costs to a minimum. Further, the Committee directs the USCP to continue to provide a detailed report to the Committee within 60 days of issuance of this report on the Department's efforts to manage the use of overtime on a quarterly basis. Additionally, the USCP should provide a written report that provides updated detailed and specific recommendations for addressing the USCP's requests for implementation of 1811 classification status, the implementation of availability pay and inclusion of overtime within retirement calculation methodology. The report should speak to the costs and benefits of implementing these recommendations, including the expected operational mission benefits and the costs of implementation and out-year fiscal impacts from each. Prior to making any sworn staffing classification adjustments that may impact salaries and overtime, the USCP should consult with the Office of Personnel Management, the Department of Justice, the Congressional Budget Office and other relevant Federal entities to develop the report and input from these agencies should be included in the report's details.
unchanged since FY2025The recommendation does not include funds for USCP overtime for requirements associated with major construction projects undertaken by the Architect of the Capitol [AOC], as those funds are provided to the AOC as part of the overall project costs for those major projects and are paid to the USCP on a reimbursable basis. The Committee expects the USCP to operate within the designated funding level for requirements associated with each project. The Committee directs the USCP to provide for any additional requirement costs beyond that allocation.
tweaked FY2027USCP Overtime and Staffing Report.--The Committee directs the USCP to continue to submit to the Committee quarterly updates as required under this heading in Senate Report 118-60. The Committee expects the reports to include relevant observations from and actions taken by the newly created staffing office and to include updates on the Department's implementation of management controls over the impact of overtime on employees reaching the maximum annual payable rate. Further, the updates should include details on the Department's efforts to realign, as necessary sworn personnel to balance the use of overtime within the total required staffing requirements to meet mission.
added FY2027Review of Potential Enhancements to the USCP Retirement System.--The Committee recognizes the importance of maintaining a robust Capitol Police workforce. To do so, the USCP must have dynamic recruitment and retention tools, to include competitive retirement options, to remain a Federal workplace of choice. Therefore, the Committee directs the Government Accountability Office [GAO] to work with the USCP and other relevant parties to review potential options and provide recommendations for the enhancement of the USCP retirement system. These options should include, but are not limited to, the inclusion of overtime pay in the annuity calculations for sworn personnel, the utilization of Law Enforcement Availability Pay, and the assignment of the 1811 job classification series. The GAO shall submit a comprehensive report on recommended potential enhancements to the USCP retirement system within 180 days of the issuance of this report. The report should include the cost estimates, timelines and implementation strategy for each recommendation. The GAO is directed to coordinate in developing this report with the United States Capitol Police [USCP], the Capitol Police Board, the USCP Labor Committee, and the Office of Personnel Management, as well as other pertinent entities dependent on the nature of the recommendations. The GAO is directed to provide the Committee with updates throughout the development of this report.
